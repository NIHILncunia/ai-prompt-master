# 5. 반실사 종족 표현 라이브러리

## Purpose

A race or species is a visual anatomy framework.

It controls:

- anatomy type
- body proportions
- head and face structure
- surface material
- silhouette
- movement impression
- species-specific focal traits

Do not assume the subject is human.
Do not assume the subject has a human face.
Do not assume gender unless the user provides it or it is necessary for the concept.

## 스타일 상속

이 라이브러리는 `../../라이브러리/스타일/붓터치 반실사.md`를 스타일 정본으로 참조한다. 이 문서는 대상·문화권·프리셋·클래스·테마 데이터만 제공하며 독립적인 렌더링 화풍을 강제하지 않는다.

## Shared Species Data Source

When a species has dedicated data, consult:

```text
TRPG 데이터/1. 프롬프트/공통/종족 데이터/AGENTS.md
TRPG 데이터/1. 프롬프트/공통/종족 데이터/데이터/
```

The shared species file is the canonical anatomy source. This library only adapts that structure to realistic semi-realistic painterly fantasy rendering.

It does not override the user's explicit design description or reference-image identity. However, this folder never leaves the mandatory painterly semi-realistic boundary; explicit 2D or anime-style requests must be routed to the separate anime portrait system.


## Species Data Priority

Resolve species identity in this order:

1. the user's latest explicit description
2. the user's reference image and corrections
3. dedicated species data in `공통/종족 데이터/데이터/`
4. this generic species-expression library
5. broad model assumptions only when no other source exists

When dedicated species data exists, load it before writing the final English prompt. Do not summarize it from memory.

## Dedicated Species Data Contract

Each dedicated species file should define, where relevant:

- canonical name, Korean name, aliases, and classification
- core visual identity in one sentence
- anatomy topology and limb count
- head and face structure
- body proportions and posture
- surface materials and texture behavior
- sexual dimorphism or explicit absence of dimorphism
- age stages and size range
- mandatory visual anchors
- optional variations and subtypes
- forbidden default interpretations
- clothing, armor, and equipment adaptation
- movement and pose logic
- environment and lighting compatibility
- true form, humanoid form, polymorph form, transformed form, and other alternate-form rules
- identity traits shared across forms
- prompt reinforcement block

Use `공통/종족 데이터/템플릿/종족 시각 표현 데이터 템플릿.md` as the authoring standard.

## Multi-Form Species Doctrine

A species or individual may have multiple valid forms, including true form, humanoid form, polymorph form, beast form, armored form, awakened form, corrupted form, spirit form, or mechanical shell form.

Treat each form as an independent visual specification with:

- its own anatomy
- its own surface materials
- its own clothing or equipment logic
- its own required and forbidden traits
- shared identity anchors linking it to the same being

When the user requests a form set:

- count the requested forms and framings explicitly
- generate each requested image as a separate standalone image unless the user explicitly asks for a collage or sheet
- never merge several requested portraits into one grid, split panel, contact sheet, or composite by default
- preserve face, eye color, color language, symbols, scars, ornaments, and other shared identity anchors across forms
- do not leak anatomy from one form into another unless the species data explicitly requires it
- for polymorph forms, distinguish biological traits from armor or clothing motifs

Example: a dragon true form and a humanoid polymorph form may share eye color, scale-inspired armor, heraldry, and demeanor while the humanoid form remains anatomically human unless otherwise specified.

## Three-Dimensional Reality-First Species Rendering

Species traits must be rendered as physically present three-dimensional anatomy or material, not as decorative illustration shorthand or flat painted texture.

For humanoids, preserve realistic skeletal structure, musculature, skin response, weight, and balance. For non-humans, use zoological, biological, mechanical, mineral, botanical, or elemental plausibility appropriate to the subject.

Avoid:

- costume-like glued-on ears, horns, tails, wings, feathers, scales, or fins
- human anatomy with superficial animal accessories when a true non-human species is requested
- flat decorative textures painted onto skin
- impossible attachment points
- material transitions that look like a costume seam unless the design is actually clothing or armor
- illustration-only exaggeration that weakens physical plausibility

## Neutral Subject Rule

Use "subject" as the internal neutral term.

The subject may be:

- male
- female
- androgynous
- genderless
- humanoid
- non-humanoid
- beast
- monster
- construct
- spirit
- deity
- amorphous being
- mechanical entity
- object-like magical entity

Prompt language must adapt to the actual subject.

## Anatomy Types

### Humanoid

Traits:

- upright posture
- two arms and two legs
- human-like balance
- readable face and hands

Use for:

- humans
- elves
- dwarves
- orcs
- tieflings
- aasimar
- most playable ancestries
- humanoid beastfolk

### Quadrupedal

Traits:

- four-legged locomotion
- beast posture
- animal balance
- head-forward silhouette

Use for:

- wolves
- tigers
- lions
- guardian beasts
- stone animals
- draconic beasts
- non-humanoid monsters

Important:

- Do not force a humanoid torso unless requested.

### Avian

Traits:

- feathers
- beak or bird-like face if relevant
- wings or wing-like arms if relevant
- lightweight silhouette
- talons or bird-like feet

Use for:

- aarakocra
- harpies
- bird spirits
- celestial birds
- avian monsters

Dedicated data:

```text
TRPG 데이터/1. 프롬프트/공통/종족 데이터/데이터/DND/Aarakocra.md
```

### Serpentine

Traits:

- elongated body
- flexible spine
- coils
- scale texture
- flowing motion

Use for:

- nagas
- serpent spirits
- dragons with long bodies
- snake monsters

### Draconic

Traits:

- scales
- horns
- snout or draconic facial planes
- claws
- tail
- wings if specified
- powerful reptilian silhouette

Use for:

- dragonborn
- dragons
- drakes
- dragon spirits
- draconic humanoids

### Insectoid

Traits:

- exoskeleton
- segmented limbs
- mandibles or insect head if relevant
- chitin plates
- alien posture

### Aquatic

Traits:

- fins
- gills
- webbing
- scales
- smooth wet surfaces
- fish, eel, crab, squid, octopus, jellyfish, or deep-sea motifs

Use for:

- merfolk

- aquatic monsters

### Amorphous

Traits:

- unstable outline
- gelatinous body
- liquid or mist-like form
- shifting silhouette

Important:

### Construct

Traits:

- artificial anatomy
- mechanical, stone, crystal, bone, wood, or clay body
- visible joints or carved segments
- rigid or ritualized movement

Use for:

- golems
- animated armor
- mechanical soldiers
- warforged-like beings
- droids
- arcane constructs
- stone guardians

## Mechanical / Construct Special Doctrine

When the subject is a mechanical character, droid, machina, warforged-like entity, artificial soldier, full-body machine, or arcane-industrial construct:

Default interpretation:

- fully artificial body
- no human skin
- no soft human face
- steel mask, armored faceplate, sensor head, mono-eye, visor, carved metal face, or readable mechanical head
- the head or mask is the identity focal point

Use:

- blackened steel
- dark iron
- gunmetal
- worn brass
- arcane alloy
- ceramic metal
- bolts
- pistons
- hydraulic joints
- armored cables
- vents
- scratches
- chipped paint
- soot
- oil stains
- dust
- mechanical seams

Avoid:

- flesh-colored face
- normal human lips
- human eyes unless explicitly android
- exposed organic skin
- cyborg interpretation when full machine is requested
- sleek modern robot unless requested
- science fiction drift when fantasy construct is requested

If the user explicitly requests an android or human-like artificial being:

- human-like face may be allowed
- preserve artificial signs through seams, synthetic skin, visible joints, or subtle mechanical details

## Surface Trait Library

### Smooth Skin

Use:

- realistic skin texture
- pores and subtle color variation
- believable facial planes

Avoid:

- plastic skin
- doll-like over-smoothing

### Fur

Use:

- directional fur flow
- layered texture
- species-specific color pattern
- clear transition between skin and fur if hybrid

### Scales

Use:

- scale pattern following body structure
- larger plates on armor-like areas
- smaller scales around joints
- natural color variation

### Feathers

Use:

- layered feather groups
- readable wing structure
- crest, plumage, or tail feathers if relevant

### Chitin

Use:

- segmented plates
- glossy or matte insect shell
- joint articulation
- organic armor structure

### Stone

Use:

- cracked rock texture
- carved planes
- weathered edges
- moss or dust if ancient
- heavy mass

### Crystal

Use:

- translucent facets
- internal glow if magical
- sharp geometry
- reflected light

### Metal

Use:

- distinct alloys
- scratches
- dents
- edge wear
- bolts and seams
- polished or worn finish as requested

### Bone

Use:

- dry texture
- cracks
- ivory or yellowed tones

### Shadow / Mist

- semi-solid focal structure

- readable head or core

### Merfolk / Aquatic Humanoid

## Undead

### Skeleton

Use:

- bone structure
- skull focal point
- armor or robes if classed
- glowing eyes only if appropriate

### Zombie / Corpse

Use:

- decayed anatomy
- torn clothing
- asymmetry
- visible rot

Avoid:

- gore overload unless requested

### Vampire

Use:

- aristocratic or predatory face
- pale skin
- refined or monstrous traits depending on concept
- fangs only if visible and appropriate

### Ghost / Spirit

Use:

- translucent edges
- pale light
- semi-visible body
- clear face or focal point

## Spirits and Deities

For minor deities, spirits, avatars, and divine beings:

- preserve the domain visually
- use symbolic materials
- keep body readable
- do not overexpose the figure with glow
- avoid automatically changing gender presentation

If a deity is described as male but appearing in a feminine form, preserve both:

- male deity identity in concept
- feminine manifested appearance in visual form

## Monster / Creature Rendering

For monsters:

- prioritize silhouette
- define head, limbs, torso, and tail clearly
- use focal anatomy
- keep limb count readable
- support scale through framing and environmental cues
